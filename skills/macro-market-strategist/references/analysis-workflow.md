# Macro Market Strategy Workflow

## Source hierarchy

- Central banks: Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, TCMB and other relevant central banks.
- Official statistical agencies: BLS, BEA, Census, Eurostat, Destatis, TUIK, ONS, national agencies.
- Fiscal and debt sources: Treasury departments, finance ministries, IMF, World Bank, OECD.
- Market sources: yield curves, breakevens, FX, credit spreads, equity indices, commodity curves.
- Survey sources: ISM, S&P Global PMI, NFIB, consumer confidence, lending surveys.

## Research process

1. Define geography, asset universe, time horizon, and whether the user wants nowcast, regime, policy path, or trade implication.
2. Gather current facts:
   - Inflation: headline/core, services, shelter, wages, expectations.
   - Growth: GDP, PMIs, industrial production, retail sales, credit impulse.
   - Labor: payrolls, unemployment, participation, quits, wages.
   - Policy: current rate, expected path, balance sheet, fiscal impulse.
   - Markets: 2y/10y rates, curve shape, real yields, FX, credit spreads, equity breadth, commodities.
3. Classify regime:
   - Goldilocks: growth stable, inflation falling, liquidity supportive.
   - Reflation: growth and inflation accelerating.
   - Stagflation: growth weakening, inflation sticky.
   - Disinflationary slowdown: growth and inflation weakening.
   - Crisis/liquidity shock: funding stress dominates macro data.
4. Compare market pricing to macro path:
   - What is priced in rates, FX, equities, credit, commodities?
   - Where is the biggest mismatch between data, policy guidance, and positioning?
5. Map cross-asset impact:
   - Equities: earnings cycle, multiple pressure, sector rotation.
   - Rates: front-end policy expectations, long-end term premium.
   - FX: rate differentials, external balance, reserve/liquidity stress.
   - Credit: default risk, spreads, refinancing wall.
   - Commodities: demand cycle, supply shock, inventory cycle.
6. Produce scenarios with triggers and invalidation points.

## Watchpoints

- Macro data is revised; note preliminary versus final data.
- Market reaction depends on surprise versus expectations, not just the absolute number.
- Central bank reaction functions change by regime.
- Liquidity and positioning can dominate fundamentals in short windows.
- Country macro must include currency, reserves, current account, inflation credibility, and external funding needs.
